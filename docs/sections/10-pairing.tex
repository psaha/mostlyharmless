\section{The coefficient pairing}
\label{sec:pairing}

A map and its visibility are expanded with the \emph{same} stored coefficients, and by the
same pairing.  This section collects that correspondence in one place, because it is used by
every later section, it is easy to get wrong, and the two bases do not treat negative orders
the same way.  Nothing here is new: it assembles results of
sections~\ref{sec:harmonics}, \ref{sec:wigner} and \ref{sec:visibility}.

\subsection{The map}
\label{sec:pairing-map}

The stored coefficients are those of order $m \ge 0$, and the map is real, so the negative
orders are determined rather than stored:
\begin{equation}
  a_{l,-m} \;=\; (-1)^m\,\overline{a_{lm}} .
  \label{eq:pairing-reality}
\end{equation}
Expanding over all orders and folding the negative ones back onto the stored half gives
\begin{equation}
  I(n) \;=\; \sum_{l}\Bigl[\, a_{l0}\,Y_{l0}(n)
    \;+\; \sum_{m=1}^{l}\Bigl( a_{lm}\,Y_{lm}(n)
    \;+\; (-1)^m\,\overline{a_{lm}}\,Y_{l,-m}(n) \Bigr) \Bigr] ,
  \label{eq:pairing-map}
\end{equation}
and because the harmonics obey
\begin{equation}
  Y_{l,-m} \;=\; (-1)^m\,\overline{Y_{lm}} ,
  \label{eq:pairing-y-negative}
\end{equation}
the two terms are conjugates of one another and
\begin{equation}
  I(n) \;=\; \sum_{l}\Bigl[\, a_{l0}\,Y_{l0}(n)
    \;+\; \sum_{m=1}^{l} 2\,\mathrm{Re}\bigl( a_{lm}\,Y_{lm}(n) \bigr) \Bigr] .
  \label{eq:pairing-map-real}
\end{equation}

\subsection{The visibility}
\label{sec:pairing-visibility}

The visibility is linear in the map, so applying it term by term to the stored expansion
\eqref{eq:pairing-map-real} needs one fact about the transform and nothing more: how it
treats a conjugate.  Writing the transform as $\mathcal{F}$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{F}\bigl[\,\overline{f}\,\bigr](u,v)
  \;=\; \overline{\mathcal{F}[f](-u,-v)} ,
  \label{eq:transform-conjugate}
\end{equation}
which says that conjugating a map conjugates its transform \emph{and} mirrors the baseline,
a plane wave with its phase conjugated being the wave travelling the other way.  No negative
order appears in that statement, and none is needed to apply it: the second half of
$2\,\mathrm{Re}(a_{lm}Y_{lm}) = a_{lm}Y_{lm} + \overline{a_{lm}}\,\overline{Y_{lm}}$ is a
conjugate, and \eqref{eq:transform-conjugate} is all there is to know about its transform.

What the mirror does to a single mode is a phase.  Reversing both components of the baseline
sends $\psi \to \psi + \pi$ in \eqref{eq:mode-visibility}, and the order-$m$ mode picks up
$e^{im\pi} = (-1)^m$:
\begin{equation}
  V_{lm}(-u,-v) \;=\; (-1)^m\,V_{lm}(u,v) .
  \label{eq:pairing-baseline-sign}
\end{equation}
Composing the two, the conjugate of a mode transforms to
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{F}\bigl[\overline{Y_{lm}}\bigr] \;=\; (-1)^m\,\overline{V_{lm}} ,
  \label{eq:pairing-v-negative}
\end{equation}
so the stored term and its conjugate partner contribute
\begin{equation}
  a_{lm}V_{lm} + (-1)^m\,\overline{a_{lm}V_{lm}}
  \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    2\,\mathrm{Re}\bigl(a_{lm}V_{lm}\bigr) , & m \text{ even},\\[2pt]
    2i\,\mathrm{Im}\bigl(a_{lm}V_{lm}\bigr) , & m \text{ odd}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:pairing-contribution}
\end{equation}
Summing over the stored half therefore splits the inner sum by parity, which is the
visibility's counterpart of the map's \eqref{eq:pairing-map},
\begin{equation}
  V(u,v) \;=\; \sum_{l}\Bigl[\, a_{l0}\,V_{l0}(u,v)
    \;+\; \sum_{m=1}^{l}
      \begin{cases}
        2\,\mathrm{Re}\bigl( a_{lm}\,V_{lm}(u,v) \bigr) , & m \text{ even},\\[2pt]
        2i\,\mathrm{Im}\bigl( a_{lm}\,V_{lm}(u,v) \bigr) , & m \text{ odd}.
      \end{cases}
  \Bigr] .
  \label{eq:pairing-visibility-parity}
\end{equation}
That is the whole difference from the map, in one sign.  In \eqref{eq:pairing-map} the
harmonics' own $(-1)^m$ met the coefficient's and the two squared away, leaving
$2\,\mathrm{Re}$ at every order; here there is nothing for it to cancel against.

Substituting the mode sum into either branch, both reduce to the same expression, because
$(-i)^m$ is real for even $m$ and imaginary for odd $m$; each multiplies its branch into a
common form,
\begin{equation}
  a_{lm}V_{lm} + (-1)^m\,\overline{a_{lm}V_{lm}}
  \;=\; 4\pi(-i)^m N_{lm}R_{lm}(q)\,\mathrm{Re}\bigl(a_{lm}e^{im\psi}\bigr) ,
  \label{eq:pairing-compact}
\end{equation}
which is the contribution quoted in section~\ref{sec:visibility}.  So
\begin{equation}
  V(u,v) \;=\; \sum_{l}\Bigl[\, a_{l0}\,V_{l0}(u,v)
    \;+\; \sum_{m=1}^{l} 4\pi(-i)^m N_{lm}R_{lm}(q)\,
      \mathrm{Re}\bigl( a_{lm}\,e^{im\psi} \bigr) \Bigr] .
  \label{eq:pairing-visibility-real}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Why the parity survives the transform}
\label{sec:pairing-why}

Equation~\eqref{eq:pairing-y-negative} is a definition of the basis, and it is what makes the
map's inner sum carry no parity at all: the harmonic's own $(-1)^m$ meets the coefficient's
and the two square away, leaving $2\,\mathrm{Re}$ at every order.

For the visibility there is only \emph{one} sign to be had.  Conjugation contributes none,
\eqref{eq:transform-conjugate} being a plain conjugation at the mirrored baseline, and the
mirror contributes $(-1)^m$.  Nothing meets it, so nothing cancels it, and it survives as the
switch between real and imaginary parts in \eqref{eq:pairing-contribution}.  That is the whole
reason the map carries a $(-1)^m$ and the visibility does not, and the reason the mode sum
shows a $(-i)^m$ phase at all.

\subsection{What the pairing buys}
\label{sec:pairing-buys}

Three consequences, each used constantly:

\begin{itemize}
  \item only $m \ge 0$ is stored, halving the coefficient set, and half of what remains is
    real ($a_{l0}$);
  \item each stored coefficient costs exactly one mode evaluation, and the mode is a closed
    form (section~\ref{sec:odd} or~\ref{sec:even}), so the cost of a visibility is set by
    the number of modes and not by any sampling;
  \item the pairing is unchanged by rotation, since \eqref{eq:coefficient-rotation} delivers
    observer-frame coefficients in the same half-complex form.  A rotated map therefore
    needs no re-derivation, only a new coefficient set.
\end{itemize}

Identity \eqref{eq:pairing-baseline-sign} is \textbf{certificate \vtag{23}}: it is checked
directly against \eqref{eq:mode-visibility} at the two baselines, with no coefficients and no
pairing involved, so the identity is isolated rather than inferred.  That isolation is the
point.  The Hermitian symmetry of a real map, $V(-u,-v) = \overline{V(u,v)}$, also depends on
it, but only in combination with the pairing, so a sign error common to both would satisfy
the symmetry and still be wrong.  Certificate \vtag{23} would fail instead, and it would fail
loudly if the sense of the azimuth were ever changed again --- which it has been.
