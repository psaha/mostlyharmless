\section{Stellar interferometry}

One is interested in the interferometric visibility, which is the
Fourier transform of the brightness map on the sky.  Standard usage
take the following form.
\begin{equation}
  V(u,v) = \int I(x,y)\, e^{-2\pi i (u x + v y)}\; dx\, dy
  \label{eq:visibility-definition}
\end{equation}
If we have a three-dimensional model with brightness defined on a
surface we have
\begin{equation}
  V(u,v) = \int_{\uvecn\cdot\uvecz > 0}
  I(\vecr\in S)\, 
  e^{-2\pi i (u x + v y)}\; (\uvecn\cdot\uvecz)\, dS
  \label{eq:deformation-visibility-integral}
\end{equation}
Here $\uvecn$ denotes a unit vector normal to the surface.

For a spinning a triaxially deformed star we want to model the
brightness and interferometric visibility in the following form.
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \tilde I(x,y) &= \sum_{lm} a_{lm} \Yup_{lm}\left(\Lambda^{-1}\circ(x,y)\right) \\
    \tilde V(u,v) &= \sum_{lm} a_{lm} V_{lm}\left(\Lambda\circ(x,y)\right)
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Our goals
\begin{itemize}
  \item Find suitable basis functions $\Yup_{lm}(x,y)$ on a circle
    along with their Fourier transforms $V_lm(u,v)$.  Solved by \cite{2026PASP..138e4504D}.
  \item Find an affine transformation $\Lambda$.
\end{itemize}
